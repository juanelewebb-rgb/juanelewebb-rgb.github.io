About

My path into engineering started with food. Growing up, I didn’t always have access to the quality of food I wanted, and as I got older I thought about what I wanted to change for my younger siblings and, one day, my own children. I found hydroponics and vertical towers on Instagram by chance and began to imagine on-demand produce stores in urban communities. That curiosity became Project OSHN, a growing system idea that could give people more control over where and how their food is produced.

OSHN is what brought me back to school. Every step forward showed me something I didn’t know yet: how the system works mechanically, how plants respond to their environment, where the energy comes from, and how materials could change what the system can do. I wanted to understand those problems deeply enough to build solutions to them.

I’m most drawn to problems without an obvious answer. I like asking why something works, where it falls short, and what happens if I try it differently. Research gives me room to investigate uncertain ideas and turn what I learn into something useful. Equitable food access is still personal to me, but my mission is bigger now: using engineering and research to create practical solutions that improve how people live.
