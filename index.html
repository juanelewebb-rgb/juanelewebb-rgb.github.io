About

My journey into engineering started with food. Growing up, I didn’t always have access to the quality of food I wanted, and as I got older, I started thinking about what I wanted to change for my younger siblings and, one day, my own children. I came across hydroponics and vertical towers on Instagram by chance, and I began to dream of on-demand produce grocery stores in urban communities. What started as curiosity eventually became Project OSHN, an idea for a growing system that could give people more control over where and how their food is produced.

OSHN is what brought me back to school. I had an idea I believed in, but every step forward seemed to uncover something I didn’t know yet. I wanted to understand the mechanics of the system, how plants respond to their environment, where the energy comes from, and how materials could change what the system was capable of doing. Eventually, I realized that if I wanted to build solutions to problems I cared about, I needed the education to understand those problems deeply enough to do it well.

I’ve realized that I’m most drawn to problems that don’t already have an obvious answer. I like questioning why something works the way it does, where it falls short, and what might happen if I approach it differently. That curiosity is what pulled me toward research. It gives me the freedom to investigate ideas that are still uncertain, learn from what doesn’t work, and eventually turn what I learn into something useful.

Equitable food access is still deeply personal to me, but my mission has grown beyond food. I want to use engineering and research to create practical solutions that improve how people live. That purpose is what brought me back to school, and it continues to shape what I choose to learn and what I hope to build.
